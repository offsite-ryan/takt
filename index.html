What TAKT is

TAKT rolls up daily production data — entered manually or fed by an API — into standardized OEE, TEEP, Yield, and CoQ figures across any number of locations and areas. Instead of every plant reporting these numbers in its own spreadsheet with its own assumptions, everyone feeds the same calculation and rolls up to one comparable view.

Custom adapters can be built to connect TAKT to your existing systems — MES, ERP, a historian, whatever's already running the floor — so the numbers populate automatically instead of requiring manual entry. The pencil icon is always available to correct or fill in a value by hand when something needs a human override.

Where the name comes from

"Takt" isn't an acronym — it's the German word for a musical beat or pulse. In lean manufacturing, takt time is the pace a line needs to run at to match customer demand: available production time ÷ demand. It's one of the foundational concepts in lean production, alongside metrics like OEE and cycle time — the app is named after the underlying rhythm it's built to track.

Why it's worth doing

A one-point improvement in OEE at a typical mid-size plant is commonly worth a meaningful six-figure sum annually in reduced downtime, scrap, and overtime — the hard part is usually not knowing where to focus first. Standardizing how every location measures and reports the same four numbers is what makes that focus possible at a corporate level:

Comparability — locations stop being measured against their own history in isolation and start being measured against each other and against a shared benchmark.
Faster triage — an underperforming line shows up immediately in a rollup instead of surfacing weeks later in a monthly report.
Less manual reporting overhead — one shared model replaces however many spreadsheet variants currently exist across sites.
Accountability — area-level benchmark targets turn "how are we doing" into a specific, visible number instead of a subjective read.

OEE — Overall Equipment Effectiveness

OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality

Availability = Run Time ÷ Planned Time, where Run Time = Planned Time − Downtime. Performance = (Total Units × Ideal Cycle Time) ÷ Run Time — how close output ran to the ideal rate. Quality = Good Units ÷ Total Units.

Industry benchmark: 85%+ is generally considered "world class." 40–60% is typical for manufacturers who haven't focused on OEE improvement yet; 60–85% is a solid, actively-managed operation. The commonly cited world-class split is roughly 90% Availability × 95% Performance × 99% Quality ≈ 85% OEE.

TEEP — Total Effective Equipment Performance

TEEP = OEE × 24/7 Utilization

Utilization = Planned Time ÷ Calendar Time (1,440 minutes in a day). TEEP measures OEE against all available time, not just scheduled time — so it's usually lower than OEE.

Industry benchmark: there's no single standard figure the way OEE has 85% — TEEP depends heavily on how many shifts a site chooses to run. A single-shift operation at a world-class 85% OEE only reaches roughly 35% TEEP just from running ~1/3 of the calendar day; the number matters more as a trend over time, or as a case for adding capacity, than as a fixed target.

Yield

Yield = Good Units ÷ Total Units × 100

The share of units produced that were good on the first pass. It's the same ratio used as Quality inside the OEE formula above.

Industry benchmark: varies widely by sector — Six Sigma quality (99.99966%) is the aspirational ceiling, general discrete manufacturing often targets 95–99%, and anything trending under ~90% is usually treated as a quality escape worth investigating.

CoQ — Cost of Quality

CoQ = Cost of Poor Quality ÷ Revenue × 100

The cost of scrap, rework, and defects as a percentage of revenue. Unlike the other three metrics, lower is better here.

Industry benchmark: world-class organizations typically run CoQ around 1–2% of revenue; 5–10% is common for an average operation; above ~15% usually signals a systemic quality problem worth prioritizing.

The cards below the chart

The four cards under the chart change based on which view is active — they're not always "Current / Average / Best / Worst."

Trend — Current (with the change vs the prior period), Period Average, Best, and Worst for the selected metric.
Locations / Overview — Best Location and Worst Location (with their values), Spread (the gap between them), and On Target (how many locations are currently at or beyond the benchmark).
Table — Net Change from the first period shown to the last, and On Target (how many periods were at or beyond the benchmark).
Compare — Needs Attention (whichever of the four metrics has the biggest gap from its own target right now) and On Target (how many of the four metrics are currently meeting theirs).
Today — hidden entirely; this view is a status board of raw inputs, not a performance summary, so a metric-based card row wouldn't fit it.

Combining locations into a group

Each location's OEE/TEEP/Yield/CoQ is computed once, when that day is saved, and never recalculated afterward — so a formula change later never rewrites a past day's number. When more than one location is selected, TAKT combines those already-computed numbers by weighting each location's metrics by its Planned Time for that day, so a line that was scheduled to run longer counts for more in the group number. A simple average instead would let a small line's number swing the group total as much as a big one.

Edit vs. Schedule

Two buttons open this same dialog, for two different jobs. Edit (pencil icon) records what already happened — its date picker only reaches back through the last 14 days and never forward past today. Schedule (calendar icon) assigns upcoming work ahead of time — its date picker only reaches forward, from today through days out, and never into the past. Both open the exact same fields; which ones you actually see depends on the date you land on, not which button you clicked — see "Scheduling ahead," below.

Date and location

The seven inputs

The table is split into two groups, same as in the modal itself:

Daily Actuals — what the operator actually observed on the floor; update these every time.
Up Time — hours the line actually ran; you enter this one directly.
Downtime — calculated automatically as Planned Time − Up Time (Up Time is converted to minutes first), not typed in directly, so the two can never disagree.
Total Units — calculated as Up Time ÷ Ideal Cycle Time (again converting Up Time to minutes to match Ideal Cycle Time's units): how many units should have come off the line in the time it actually ran.
Good Units — units that passed quality; capped at Total Units. When Up Time changes, this defaults to match Total Units (assuming 100% quality) until someone actually types a number in — see the Saving note below for why that matters.
Reason for Downtime — optional; one of 5 reasons configured per area under Settings, for whenever there was downtime — describes why the line stopped, not why a part failed quality (that's what Good Units measures independently). What gets saved is a stable code (e.g. "T1"), not the text you see in the dropdown — so renaming a reason's wording in Settings later doesn't disconnect it from records already saved against it.

Planned / Target — set periodically, usually by planning or finance rather than the operator entering daily production.
Planned Time — planned production minutes for the day.
Ideal Cycle Time — minutes per unit at the ideal rate for this location.
Cost of Poor Quality ($) — cost of scrap, rework, and defects for the day.
Revenue ($) — revenue for the day, used as CoQ's denominator.
Assigned To — who's responsible for this location's plan; free text, purely informational and doesn't affect any calculation.
Priority — Low/Medium/High/Critical; also informational only.

Scheduling ahead

Pick a date later than today (only reachable via Schedule) and Daily Actuals disappears entirely — there's nothing to observe yet on a day that hasn't happened. Only Planned / Target shows, which is exactly where Assigned and Priority live, so a scheduler can staff out upcoming days without touching anything an operator would later record. Once that date arrives, the Today view shows whatever was assigned automatically — no extra step needed.

Live preview

The panel below the table recalculates OEE, TEEP, Yield, and CoQ as you type, using the same formulas shown on the Metrics tab — so you can see the effect of an edit before saving it.

Saving

Save writes this one location's numbers for this one date. It doesn't touch any other location or day. In Sample mode, Week/Month/YTD trends are separate synthetic data layered around it; in Live/API mode they're real, aggregated straight from saved days like this one.

Opening the Edit modal, or changing its date, fetches that day's latest record first — so you're always starting from current data, not whatever was on screen when the page loaded. Save always sends the complete record (last save wins); there's no field-level merge, so the fetch-on-open is what keeps you from editing stale numbers.

OEE/TEEP/Yield/CoQ are computed once, right at the moment you save, from the values being saved — and stored alongside them from then on. They're never recalculated later, so a formula change down the road only affects new saves; a past day's numbers stay exactly as they were the day they were entered.

Today view

A quick, one-row-per-location table for today only — Planned Time, Assigned, Priority, Up Time, Down Time, and Reason for Downtime. Good Units isn't shown here; it's validated later via the Edit modal.

Read-only by default and kept current automatically. Click Edit to fetch the latest data and unlock the row inputs. Changes are staged locally as you type — nothing is sent anywhere until you click Done Editing, which saves every row you touched in one batch (one save per changed row, not per field).

If a row fails to save, it's flagged red and stays editable — the table won't drop back to read-only until every changed row has saved successfully. Click Done Editing again to retry just the rows still outstanding.

Even though this table doesn't show them, saving here still recomputes that location's OEE/TEEP/Yield/CoQ — Planned Time and Down Time changes here do affect them — using whatever Good Units figure that day already had on file.

Data source

Switches TAKT between generated sample data and a real backend. In Live API mode, set the API base URL — every read and save goes through that endpoint instead of local sample generation. If a request fails, TAKT quietly falls back to sample data so the demo keeps working.

API Reference

Two endpoints, both under the base URL set above. {locationId} is a location's code (e.g. "L001"), and {date} (YYYY-MM-DD) is a path parameter.

MethodPathPurpose
POST/takt/locations/daysBulk read — any number of dates × any number of locations in one call. Covers a single record, one day across many locations (Today view), and many days across many locations (history/trends) — called once at startup, and again whenever an editor opens or its date changes
PATCH/takt/locations/{locationId}/days/{date}Save one day's full record for one location (a complete overwrite, not a field-level merge — see the note below)

Bulk read request body:

{ dates: [...], locations: [...] }

locations is a list of location codes. The response is nested date, then location:

{ "<date>": { "<locationCode>": record | null } }

A null means no record was ever saved for that location/date — TAKT never fabricates a number for it; the chart shows a real gap there instead.

Record shape (each value in the bulk read's response, and the PATCH body):

{ planned, downtime, totalUnits, goodUnits, idealCycleTime, coqCost, revenue, scrapReason, scrapReasonText, assigned, priority, metrics, benchmarks }

metrics — { OEE, TEEP, Yield, CoQ } — is computed by TAKT, not the backend. Every PATCH includes it already calculated from the rest of that same request body; the backend just stores and returns it unchanged, same as every other field. This keeps the formula in one place (this app) and means a past day's numbers never shift if the formula changes later — only new saves pick up a change.

scrapReason is a stable code (e.g. "T1") a downstream report can key off permanently, even after ops relabels it later. scrapReasonText is that code's display text, resolved once against the matching area's Scrap Reasons list (see the Settings tab) and snapshotted onto the record at save time — like metrics, TAKT computes it and every PATCH includes it already resolved. This is deliberate: teams and terminology change over a year, so a record saved today keeps showing what "T5" meant on the day it was saved, not whatever it's since been redefined to mean.

benchmarks — { OEE, TEEP, Yield, CoQ } — is the same idea, for the record's area's target values (Settings > Area Defaults > Benchmarks): snapshotted at save time, so a record keeps showing what it was actually being measured against that day, even after targets get revised later. This field is purely historical/analytical — TAKT's own dashed benchmark line and "over/under target" text always compare against current settings, never this frozen copy.

PATCH always sends the complete record, not just the changed fields — there's no server-side merge to rely on. TAKT always fetches the latest record before an edit begins (opening the Edit modal, changing its date, or turning on Today's edit toggle), so it's always writing on top of current data; whichever save lands last wins.

If a request fails or times out, TAKT falls back to sample data for that read rather than showing an error — worth knowing if data looks unexpectedly like the demo defaults while testing a real endpoint.

The Token field in Settings > Data source is sent as an X-Tenant-Token header on every request above, in addition to the Content-Type header — a long-lived, manually-issued credential rather than a short-lived SSO bearer token. Leave it blank if your backend doesn't need one.

Areas

A grouping layer above locations. Add or delete areas here, and reassign a location to a different area from its Area field below. On the main screen, clicking an area chip selects every location that belongs to it in one click — a shortcut for the Group filter, not a separate calculation.

Benchmarks

Each area has its own target for OEE, TEEP, Yield, and CoQ, shown as a dashed reference line on the Trend and Locations charts. They default to the industry rule-of-thumb figures from the Metrics tab, but every area can override them — useful since, for example, a reasonable TEEP target depends heavily on how many shifts that site runs.

If your current Group selection spans more than one area, the line shown is the average of every represented area's target — most of the time a group is a single area, so this rarely comes up.

Scrap Reasons

Five reasons, pulled from the "Machine" category of the standard 6M root-cause framework, offered as a dropdown in the Edit modal whenever there was downtime — they describe why the line stopped, distinct from Good Units, which measures quality on its own. Each has a Code (short, stable — what a billing or accounting report would key off) and a Text label (freely reworded any time, without breaking the link to records saved under that code). Each area can customize both — the count stays fixed at five.

Priority Options

The urgency levels offered for Priority in the Edit modal and Today view. Unlike scrap reasons, this is one shared list rather than per-area, and you can add or remove levels — it isn't fixed at a specific count. Sample data and the default selection both bias toward whichever level sits in the middle of the list.

Area Defaults

Planned Time, Ideal Cycle Time, Total Units Target, CoQ Cost Baseline, and Revenue Baseline — one set per area. These are what generate the numbers for any date that hasn't been individually edited yet (typically upcoming dates), so a whole area updates from one place instead of editing location by location.

The moment a specific date is edited (Edit Data dialog), that day's saved record is what it is from then on — changing these defaults later won't touch it. There's no per-location override by design: a location that genuinely needs different numbers belongs in its own area.

Locations

Pick an area, then a location, to rename it or move it to a different area. Location Name is shown throughout the app; Area is which area's defaults it uses (see Area Defaults, above) and which group filters include it.

Once you're on Live API or a date has real entered figures, the area defaults step aside — they only shape generated sample data and the placeholder shown for dates nobody has touched yet.
