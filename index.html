What TAKT is

TAKT rolls up daily production data — entered manually or fed by an API — into standardized OEE, Yield, CoQ, and Delta figures across any number of locations and areas. Instead of every plant reporting these numbers in its own spreadsheet with its own assumptions, everyone feeds the same calculation and rolls up to one comparable view.

Custom adapters can be built to connect TAKT to your existing systems — MES, ERP, a historian, whatever's already running the floor — so the numbers populate automatically instead of requiring manual entry. The pencil icon is always available to correct or fill in a value by hand when something needs a human override.

Where the name comes from

"Takt" isn't an acronym — it's the German word for a musical beat or pulse. In lean manufacturing, takt time is the pace a line needs to run at to match customer demand: available production time ÷ demand. It's one of the foundational concepts in lean production, alongside metrics like OEE and cycle time — the app is named after the underlying rhythm it's built to track.

Why it's worth doing

A one-point improvement in OEE at a typical mid-size plant is commonly worth a meaningful six-figure sum annually in reduced downtime, scrap, and overtime — the hard part is usually not knowing where to focus first. Standardizing how every location measures and reports the same four numbers is what makes that focus possible at a corporate level:

Comparability — locations stop being measured against their own history in isolation and start being measured against each other and against a shared benchmark.
Faster triage — an underperforming line shows up immediately in a rollup instead of surfacing weeks later in a monthly report.
Less manual reporting overhead — one shared model replaces however many spreadsheet variants currently exist across sites.
Accountability — area-level benchmark targets turn "how are we doing" into a specific, visible number instead of a subjective read.

OEE — Overall Equipment Effectiveness

OEE = Units % × Yield

A simplified, two-factor version of OEE — not the full textbook Availability × Performance × Quality calculation. TAKT doesn't track machine uptime or cycle time separately, so there's no true Availability or Performance split here; Units % (Done ÷ Plan) stands in for both at once. Think of it as the gain you actually captured against what was planned, scaled down by quality. Uncapped: a day that beat Plan reads over 100% OEE, same as Units % itself — that overperformance is real work, so it counts as a gain instead of being thrown away. Yield = 100% − (Scrap ÷ Done), same formula as the Yield metric below.

Industry benchmark: the familiar "85%+ is world class" figure is for the full three-factor calculation, so treat it as a rough reference point here, not a literal target — this simplified version will generally read a bit differently. 40–60% is typical for manufacturers who haven't focused on improvement yet; 60–85% is a solid, actively-managed operation.

Examples: a 200-parts-a-day station that hits its count with no issues and keeps running shouldn't have that extra output zeroed out. An hours-based testbed left running unmanned past its 8-hour shift is genuinely producing more, not "overachieving" against a bad Plan. Uncapped OEE reflects both correctly.

Yield

Yield = 100% − (Scrap ÷ Done)

The share of what was Done that was good, not scrapped. Good is derived as Done − Scrap — it's never entered directly.

Industry benchmark: varies widely by sector — Six Sigma quality (99.99966%) is the aspirational ceiling, general discrete manufacturing often targets 95–99%, and anything trending under ~90% is usually treated as a quality escape worth investigating.

CoQ — Cost of Quality

CoQ = Scrap × $ / Per

A raw dollar figure — what the day's scrap actually cost, at that area's $ / Per rate. Lower is better here (like Delta, below), and unlike the pre-rebuild version of this metric, it's not a percentage of revenue — it scales with how much an area produces, so it's most meaningful read as a trend for one location or area over time rather than compared as a flat number across very different-sized locations.

Benchmark: there's no fixed industry figure for a raw dollar cost — set each area's CoQ benchmark (Settings → Benchmarks) from that area's own history, and watch it trend down rather than judging it against a universal target.

Delta — Shortfall vs. Plan

Delta = 100% − Units %

How far a location ran behind (or ahead of) Plan, as a percentage — the same Units % used in the OEE calculation above, just flipped so a bigger Delta means a bigger miss. Lower is better, and it can go negative: a location that beat its Plan for the day shows a negative Delta. This is the trend view for what the Today table shows per-location, per-day as a raw amount (its own Down Time read-out) — Delta normalizes that same idea to a percentage so it stays comparable across hrs and qty areas and combines cleanly when a group spans more than one location.

Benchmark: no fixed industry figure here either — a location's normal day-to-day shortfall depends on how tightly its Plan is set. Start from that area's own history (Settings → Benchmarks) and watch the trend, the same as CoQ.

The cards below the chart

The four cards under the chart change based on which view is active — they're not always "Current / Average / Best / Worst."

Trend — Current (with the change vs the prior period), Period Average, Best, and Worst for the selected metric.
Locations / Overview — Best Location and Worst Location (with their values), Spread (the gap between them), and On Target (how many locations are currently at or beyond the benchmark).
Compare — Needs Attention (whichever of the four metrics has the biggest gap from its own target right now) and On Target (how many of the four metrics are currently meeting theirs).
Today — hidden entirely; this view is a status board of raw inputs, not a performance summary, so a metric-based card row wouldn't fit it.

Combining locations into a group

Each location's OEE/Yield/CoQ/Delta is computed once, when that day is saved, and never recalculated afterward — so a formula change later never rewrites a past day's number. When more than one location is selected, TAKT combines those already-computed numbers by weighting each location's metrics by its Plan for that day, so a line with a bigger plan counts for more in the group number. A simple average instead would let a small line's number swing the group total as much as a big one. Combining an "hrs" location's Plan with a "qty" location's Plan this way is a known rough edge — the weight numbers aren't the same unit — so read a mixed-type group's combined number as a rough signal, not an exact figure, and prefer filtering to one area type when it matters.

Edit vs. scheduling ahead

Edit (pencil icon) records what already happened — its date picker reaches back through the last 14 days and never forward past today. To assign upcoming work, use the calendar button next to Today: pick a date up to days out (or a past day) and the Today table shows that date — see "Scheduling ahead," below. Click the date on the tab to come back to today.

Date and location

The location list only shows whatever's currently in your Area/Group selection on the main screen — change that selection first if the location you want isn't listed.

The inputs

The table is split into two groups, same as in the modal itself:

Daily Actuals — what the operator actually observed on the floor; update these every time. Every field below is a direct, independent entry — nothing here is calculated or defaulted from another field.
Done — how much actually got done, in this location's area unit (hours or units — see Settings → Areas → Type). Never converted between the two.
Scrap — how much of Done was scrapped, same unit as Done; enter this independently of Done.
Reason — optional; one of 5 reasons configured per area under Settings, for whenever Done fell short of Plan or there was scrap — context only, it doesn't feed any calculation. What gets saved is a stable code (e.g. "T1"), not the text you see in the dropdown — so renaming a reason's wording in Settings later doesn't disconnect it from records already saved against it.
Hour Meter — the reading on the equipment's own hour meter. A plain number with no calculation attached; leave it blank if it wasn't read (blank is not the same as 0). It exists so what's reported can be checked against the physical meter at the location — trust, but verify.
Blocked — tick it when the location can't run (for example, no material). Its row gets an orange tint. It's saved with that day's record, so a new day always starts unblocked again.

Planned / Target — set periodically, usually by planning or finance rather than the operator entering daily production.
Plan — this location's target for the day, in its area's unit (hours or units). Cascades from that area's default (Settings → Area Defaults → Plan) until a specific day is saved with its own value, exactly like the rest of this section — from then on that day's saved Plan is authoritative, even if the area default changes later.
$ / Per — dollar rate per unit of Done, used to price Scrap into CoQ. Also cascades from the area default until a day overrides it.
Assigned To — who's responsible for this location's plan; free text, purely informational and doesn't affect any calculation.
Charge Code — account, job number, or charge code this day's production ties back to; free text, also purely informational.
Priority — Low/Medium/High/Critical; also informational only.

Down Time isn't a field here at all — it's always derived as max(0, Plan − Done) and shown as a read-out (Today view, charts), never entered directly. That's deliberate: it's the number this app wants a location to reduce, and a number you want reduced shouldn't also be something someone can type over.

Scheduling ahead

Pick a future date with the calendar button next to Today and the table opens up Plan, Assigned, Charge Code and Priority for editing, while Done, Scrap and Scrap Reason stay locked — there's nothing to observe yet on a day that hasn't happened. Click Edit, make your changes, then Done Editing to save. Once that date arrives, the Today view shows whatever was assigned automatically — no extra step needed.

Live preview

The panel below the table recalculates OEE, Yield, CoQ, and Delta as you type, using the same formulas shown on the Metrics tab — so you can see the effect of an edit before saving it.

Saving

Save writes this one location's numbers for this one date. It doesn't touch any other location or day. In Sample mode, Week/Month/YTD trends are separate synthetic data layered around it; in Live/API mode they're real, aggregated straight from saved days like this one.

Opening the Edit modal, or changing its date, fetches that day's latest record first — so you're always starting from current data, not whatever was on screen when the page loaded. Save always sends the complete record (last save wins); there's no field-level merge, so the fetch-on-open is what keeps you from editing stale numbers.

OEE/Yield/CoQ/Delta are computed once, right at the moment you save, from the values being saved — and stored alongside them from then on. They're never recalculated later, so a formula change down the road only affects new saves; a past day's numbers stay exactly as they were the day they were entered.

Today view

A quick, one-row-per-location table for a single day — today by default. Blocked, Plan, Done, Scrap, Hour Meter, Assigned, Charge Code, Priority, $ / Per and Reason are editable here; Delta is shown alongside as a read-only read-out (max(0, Plan − Done), in this location's own unit — hours or units) that updates live as you type Plan or Done. It's the same figure this app calls Down Time elsewhere — "Delta" is just a label that also makes sense for a qty location.

Other dates: the calendar button next to the Today tab opens a date picker — back through the last 14 days, or ahead for scheduling. The tab then shows that date in amber; click it to return to today. For a future date, Done, Scrap, Hour Meter, Blocked and Reason stay locked (there's nothing to observe yet).

Priority is a colored dot; while editing, click it to pick from a pull-down of dots (stop-light colors, low to critical — hover a dot for its name). Blocked is a checkbox that tints the whole row orange. On tablets and phones some columns are hidden to fit the screen (which ones is a site setting); every field is still editable from the Edit (pencil) dialog.

Read-only by default and kept current automatically. Click Edit to fetch the latest data and unlock the row inputs. Changes are staged locally as you type — nothing is sent anywhere until you click Done Editing, which saves every row you touched in one batch (one save per changed row, not per field).

If a row fails to save, it's flagged red, keeps what you typed, and the line above the table says why (for example, that the server couldn't be reached). The table won't drop back to read-only until every changed row has saved — click Done Editing again to retry just the rows still outstanding.

Saving here recomputes that location's OEE/Yield/CoQ/Delta from whatever Plan, Done, and Scrap are showing at that moment, same as the Edit modal.

Data source

Switches TAKT between generated sample data and a real backend. In Live API mode, set the API base URL — every read and save goes through that endpoint instead of local sample generation. If a request fails, TAKT quietly falls back to sample data so the demo keeps working.

API Reference

Two endpoints, both under the base URL set above. {locationId} is a location's code (e.g. "L001"), and {date} (YYYY-MM-DD) is a path parameter.

MethodPathPurpose
POST/takt/locations/daysBulk read — any number of dates × any number of locations in one call. Covers a single record, one day across many locations (Today view), and many days across many locations (history/trends) — called once at startup, and again whenever an editor opens or its date changes
PATCH/takt/locations/{locationId}/days/{date}Save one day's full record for one location (a complete overwrite, not a field-level merge — see the note below)

Bulk read request body:

{ dates: [...], locations: [...] }

locations is a list of location codes. The response is nested date, then location:

{ "<date>": { "<locationCode>": record | null } }

A null means no record was ever saved for that location/date — TAKT never fabricates a number for it; the chart shows a real gap there instead.

Record shape (each value in the bulk read's response, and the PATCH body):

{ plan, done, scrap, hourMeter, blocked, dollarPer, scrapReason, scrapReasonText, assigned, chargeCode, priority, metrics: { OEE, Units, Delta, Yield, CoQ }, benchmarks }

plan, done, and scrap are always in the same unit for a given location — hours for an "hrs" area, a unit count for a "qty" area (Settings > Areas > Type) — and TAKT never converts between the two. The Today view's "Delta" column and Good aren't sent at all; they're derived (max(0, plan − done) and done − scrap) and recomputed on read, never stored.

hourMeter is a number (or null when not entered) — the equipment's own hour-meter reading, stored as-is with no logic applied, so it can be checked against the physical meter. blocked is true or false for that day (a record without the field counts as not blocked); an automated process can set it by saving the complete record with it changed.

metrics — { OEE, Units, Delta, Yield, CoQ } — is computed by TAKT, not the backend. Every PATCH includes it already calculated from the rest of that same request body; the backend just stores and returns it unchanged, same as every other field. This keeps the formula in one place (this app) and means a past day's numbers never shift if the formula changes later — only new saves pick up a change. Units is the Done÷Plan attainment % — it isn't a selectable chip on its own, but it's the building block both OEE and Delta are computed from, so it's included here too.

scrapReason is a stable code (e.g. "T1") a downstream report can key off permanently, even after ops relabels it later. scrapReasonText is that code's display text, resolved once against the matching area's Scrap Reasons list (see the Settings tab) and snapshotted onto the record at save time — like metrics, TAKT computes it and every PATCH includes it already resolved. This is deliberate: teams and terminology change over a year, so a record saved today keeps showing what "T5" meant on the day it was saved, not whatever it's since been redefined to mean.

benchmarks — { OEE, Yield, CoQ, Delta } — is the same idea, for the record's area's target values (Settings > Benchmarks): snapshotted at save time, so a record keeps showing what it was actually being measured against that day, even after targets get revised later. This field is purely historical/analytical — TAKT's own dashed benchmark line and "over/under target" text always compare against current settings, never this frozen copy.

PATCH always sends the complete record, not just the changed fields — there's no server-side merge to rely on. TAKT always fetches the latest record before an edit begins (opening the Edit modal, changing its date, or turning on Today's edit toggle), so it's always writing on top of current data; whichever save lands last wins.

If a request fails or times out, TAKT falls back to sample data for that read rather than showing an error — worth knowing if data looks unexpectedly like the demo defaults while testing a real endpoint.

The Token field in Settings > Data source is sent as an X-Tenant-Token header on every request above, in addition to the Content-Type header — a long-lived, manually-issued credential rather than a short-lived SSO bearer token. Leave it blank if your backend doesn't need one.

Areas

A grouping layer above locations. Add or delete areas here, and reassign a location to a different area from its Area field below. On the main screen, clicking an area chip shows just that area, with all of its locations selected, in one click. Tick the Multi-select box at the right of the Area row to click several areas and show them together; the All button (checked-box icon) shows every area. The Group row works the same way for individual locations, with its own Multi-select box and All button. These are shortcuts for the Group filter, not a separate calculation.

Each area also has a Type — Hours or Units — set from the toggle in the Area Defaults section below. Type decides what Plan, Done, and Scrap mean for every location in that area (a duration or a unit count) and never converts between the two; pick it once per area, based on how that area's work is actually measured.

The area you pick here carries across every section below (Benchmarks, Scrap Reasons, Area Defaults, and Locations) — switch it once and all of them show that same area, rather than picking it separately in each.

Benchmarks

Each area has its own target for OEE, Yield, CoQ, and Delta, shown as a dashed reference line on the Trend and Locations charts. They default to the industry rule-of-thumb figures from the Metrics tab, but every area can override them — useful since, for example, CoQ is now a raw dollar figure, so a reasonable CoQ target depends heavily on that area's size and scrap history, not a universal percentage.

If your current Group selection spans more than one area, the line shown is the average of every represented area's target — most of the time a group is a single area, so this rarely comes up.

Scrap Reasons

Five reasons, pulled from the "Machine" category of the standard 6M root-cause framework, offered as a dropdown in the Edit modal — context for why Done fell short of Plan or why there was scrap, distinct from Good (Done − Scrap), which measures quality on its own. Each has a Code (short, stable — what a billing or accounting report would key off) and a Text label (freely reworded any time, without breaking the link to records saved under that code). Each area can customize both — the count stays fixed at five.

Priority Options

The urgency levels offered for Priority in the Edit modal and Today view. Unlike scrap reasons, this is one shared list rather than per-area, and you can add or remove levels — it isn't fixed at a specific count. A brand-new/blank record defaults to whichever level is listed first; generated sample data instead biases toward whichever level sits in the middle of the list, for a more realistic "most days are routine" spread.

Area Defaults

Type (Hours or Units), Plan, and $ / Per — one set per area. Plan and $ / Per are what generate the numbers for any date that hasn't been individually edited yet (typically upcoming dates), so a whole area updates from one place instead of editing location by location. Switching Type changes what Plan means (a duration vs. a unit count) and resets Plan to that type's own starting point — it doesn't try to convert the old value into the new unit.

The moment a specific date is edited (Edit Data dialog), that day's saved record is what it is from then on — changing these defaults later won't touch it. There's no per-location override by design: a location that genuinely needs different numbers belongs in its own area.

Locations

Pick an area, then a location, to rename it or move it to a different area. Location Name is shown throughout the app; Area is which area's defaults it uses (see Area Defaults, above) and which group filters include it.

Once you're on Live API or a date has real entered figures, the area defaults step aside — they only shape generated sample data and the placeholder shown for dates nobody has touched yet.
